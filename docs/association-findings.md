# Initial descriptive association findings

**Data policy:** the submission uses conservative cleaning of supplied records. Generator recovery has been removed. Use [the regenerated association findings](../analysis/association_analysis.md) and current dashboard for authoritative current counts and estimates.

Source: `analysis/association_analysis.json`, produced by `asteria/reporting/associations.py`. This is exploratory analysis of synthetic matched cohorts, not statistical evidence of a causal effect. Correlations are equally weighted across cohorts. Native annual CPI observations remain annual; cohort exposure means summarize employee-specific as-of context rather than inventing new measurements.

1. Six-month retention shows a weak negative unemployment association: pooled r=-0.123 across 72 country-quarter cohorts (1,092 matched hires). Requiring ten hires reduces it to -0.071; within-country centering gives -0.170. Matched retention is 87.27% versus 86.96% among 721 unmatched hires. Similar overall rates do not establish equivalent country/year composition.
2. CPI has a weak positive six-month association: r=0.162 across 30 country-hire-year cohorts (1,808 hires). Within-country centering gives 0.176; leaving out one country gives about 0.070 to 0.289. These sensitivities are not confidence limits. Time trends and other causes remain uncontrolled; this is no basis for claiming inflation improves retention.
3. Supplementary B-F vacancies show no stable directional six-month pattern: r=-0.009 across 94 cohorts, changing to +0.054 with the ten-hire check. Matched retention is 87.64% versus 85.43% among 405 unmatched hires. The matched subset and narrower industry/construction scope constrain interpretation.
4. Senior quarterly comparisons are too sparse for a confident conclusion: all 56 unemployment cohorts and all 75 B-F cohorts have fewer than ten matched hires. Unemployment-matched senior retention is 76.67% versus 81.03% unmatched, making selection particularly relevant. Annual CPI is broader but still descriptive (24 cohorts, six below ten hires).

Decision relevance: prioritize the observed senior retention shortfall and investigate internal drivers; these external comparisons provide context, not a justification for country-specific retention interventions. This is not evidence that economic conditions never matter. Better historical coverage, larger cohorts, observed internal drivers and repeated turnover windows would be needed for stronger analysis.

No p-values or statistical significance claims are made. Reused source periods/vintages and serial dependence mean cohort counts are not independent sample sizes. Country-centering handles only country means, not calendar trends or workforce composition. All prespecified available indicators are reported, avoiding selection based on attractive results. Turnover relationship estimation is deferred because the present dataset has one annual window and six country contexts; its KPI and coverage remain available.
